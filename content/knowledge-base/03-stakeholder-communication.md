---
title: Stakeholder communication
summary: Keeping product, leadership and other teams informed, especially when news is bad.
---

**Stakeholders** are the people affected by your work, or who can affect it: product managers, leadership, other engineering teams, support, legal, and sometimes customers. Good engineers make sure nobody is surprised.

**Know your audience.** A common tool is the **power/interest grid**. Manage closely the people with high influence and high interest (your PM, your director). Keep informed those with high interest but low influence (support). Each group needs different detail:

- **Leadership:** outcome, risk, date, and what you need from them.
- **Product:** scope, trade-offs and options.
- **Engineers:** the technical detail.

**Status updates** work best short and regular. Many companies use a **RAG** status (red / amber / green) plus three lines: what's done, what's next, and risks or asks.

**Delivering bad news:**

1. **Say it early.** A slip flagged two weeks ahead is planning; a slip flagged the day before is a failure.
2. **State the impact plainly.** *"Scheduled orders will be one week late."*
3. **Bring options.** *"We can ship on time without editing a scheduled order, or ship complete a week later."*
4. **Recommend one** and say what you need: *"I recommend shipping on time; I need Product to agree by Thursday."*

**Write decisions down.** A short decision log (what, why, who agreed, when) stops the same debate from happening again.

**What seniors do differently:** they translate technical problems into business impact, they never hide a problem until it has a solution, and they close the loop when it's resolved.

**In an interview:** use the "impact, options, recommendation, ask" structure to describe a time you had to share bad news.
